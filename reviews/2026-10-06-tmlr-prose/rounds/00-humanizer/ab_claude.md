## Preference

Preferred overall: **version_2**, by a **moderate** margin. Of the 34 differing paragraphs, about 25 read better in version_2, five read better in version_1, and the rest are a wash. version_2's gains are of one kind: it removes a run of pseudo-cleft constructions ("X is what makes Y", "what the exponent turns on is", "is what separates the two", "is what the rates buy"), drops editorial framing sentences that judge the paper's own work ("the paper's central measurement", "a one-line calculation", "One rule makes this work"), removes small intensifiers ("exactly", "simply", "concrete"), fixes one genuine ambiguity (L292, "where the method pays" two sentences after "pays a roughly fixed cost"), and makes the spelling consistent (the rest of the paper uses "utilization", "idealized", "synchronize", "normalizing"; version_1 alone has "centred" and "parameterisation" x4). version_1 wins where version_2 replaced a compact phrase with a wordier or looser one ("where the method has headroom" became "the part of the method with the most room for improvement") or dropped a contrast that the surrounding paragraph relies on ("a floor to clear rather than a frontier to negotiate" in the k-frontier paragraph; "Four measurements follow" before the abstract's four result sentences).

AI-likeness (0 = unmistakably human, 10 = unmistakably machine): **version_1: 3**, **version_2: 2**. Both are low: the prose is dense, specific, and bound to numbers throughout. version_1's residual signal is the cleft/"is what" tic (eight instances) and the self-framing sentences; version_2's residual signal is a few nominalized replacements ("the per-simulation gain of", "the part of the method with the most room for improvement").

## Content check

No number, result, citation, table entry or equation differs between the versions. Four differences shift emphasis or a qualification rather than wording alone; none changes a claim about the evidence:

1. L156. V1: "how faithful a stand-in it is becomes the one question of \S\ref{sec:theory}". V2: "\S\ref{sec:theory} asks how faithful a stand-in it is". V1 asserts that this is the *sole* question of the theory section; V2 only says the section asks it. Same shift at L174 (V1 "The analysis asks one question", V2 "The question is whether"). A statement about the section's scope, not about a result.

2. L251. V1: "Figure~\ref{fig:barrier} is the paper's central measurement. It covers every configuration ...". V2: "Figure~\ref{fig:barrier} covers every configuration ...". V1 ranks the result; V2 does not. Framing only.

3. L379. V1: "The effective-sample-size ceiling is where the method has headroom." V2: "The effective-sample-size ceiling is the part of the method with the most room for improvement." V2 adds a superlative ("the most") that V1 does not make. The next sentence in both versions ("Further work on this method belongs in that rule rather than in the scheduler") supports the stronger reading, so it is not unsupported, but it is a strengthening.

4. L106. V1: "One rule makes this work on a machine where workers never wait for each other: every quantity the sampler uses is recomputed from the history it is handed." V2: "Every quantity the sampler uses is recomputed from the history it is handed; this rule lets workers run without ever waiting for one another." V1 presents no-waiting as the setting the rule must work in; V2 presents it as a consequence of the rule. The introduction (L59, identical in both) uses V2's causal direction.

Everything else says the same thing in different words.
