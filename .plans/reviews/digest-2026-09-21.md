## Resolution (2026-09-21, later the same day)

Worked through in the order above; all of it is in the tex (compiles clean) and the two new
findings below came out of verifying the reviewers' claims against the raw records on scratch.

**Two findings the reviewers did not have.**

1. **The twin's "estimator collapse" is the bandwidth scheduler's throttle, not the estimator.**
   The kernel-aware search runs once per `bisect_interval` (= k) calls on *each rank's own*
   scheduler and is otherwise re-run only when that rank rebuilds its cached history after an
   out-of-order arrival (`reset_cache`). Lockstepped arrivals never trigger a rebuild, so the
   twin's bandwidth stops at its throttle points: at 20× (200 calls per rank on both arms) the
   twin reached ε = 2.9 where the matched asynchronous re-run reached 0.16, on histories whose
   ε₍₁₀₀₎ is 0.008. Re-reporting the same twin histories at ε₍₁₀₀₎ gives W1 0.010–0.015 at every
   slowdown (was 0.79 / 1.71). The heterogeneity campaign (60 s, 59 → 13 calls per rank) shows the
   same thing on *both* arms: the asynchronous arm's schedule never ran at σ ≤ 0.5 (ε stayed at
   ε₀ = 5, W1 2.1) and re-reports to 0.011. New script `make_twin_rereport.py` vendors
   `tab_twin/twin_straggler_rereport.csv` and `tab_twin_hetero/twin_hetero_rereport.csv`; Tables 3
   and 4 gained lower blocks; §7.1's paragraph is rewritten; §3.2, §7.4, §8 and the limitations
   say the schedule tightens once per k calls per rank. (The history-growth-trigger recommendation was dropped afterwards at the user's request; the rebuild mechanism is one sentence in Appendix C.) Fig. 3 is labelled as
   archive means. The plan/memory statement "the barrier itself corrupts the twin's weights" was
   wrong.
2. **The C3 boundary is a placement property.** The production campaign of the three cheap
   benchmarks ran 48 ranks as 12 per node on four nodes (the single-node submissions were
   killed); the packed single-node scaling run of the same Lotka–Volterra configuration gives
   6240 vs 2952 sims/s (ratio 2.1) where the four-node production run gives 0.96, and within the
   production run the asynchronous throughput rises from 840 to 3550 per minute. Codex's
   "run-length" reading was the symptom; the cause is placement. §7.3, Fig. 4 and Table 6
   captions, Appendix D (which wrongly said 24 ranks per high-memory node) and the limitations now
   say so.
