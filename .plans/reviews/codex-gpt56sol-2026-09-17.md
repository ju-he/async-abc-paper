**Summary**

The paper proposes a generation-free, arrival-driven ABC method combining a top-\(k\) adaptive proposal with retrospective AMIS-style weighting, and claims conditional consistency, calibration, and large HPC gains from eliminating generation barriers. The systems result is real: the committed aggregates reproduce the Gaussian SBC coverage, straggler throughput, and Cellular Potts scaling numbers, including \(9.33\to71.98\) simulations/s for the asynchronous method versus \(3.85\to14.17\) for the synchronous baseline from 48 to 384 workers. The fixed-\(m\) martingale proofs are largely coherent as conditional results. The inferential story, however, does not survive repository inspection. The principal recovery figures evaluate an unweighted top-\(k\) archive against a point mass, not the reported posterior against a posterior target; the barrier-twin script deliberately selects that diagnostic even though the committed CSV contains weighted and analytic-posterior errors that become catastrophic for the twin; the live propagator is not stateless; and the assumptions needed to connect the theorem to the asynchronous implementation are expressly unverified or violated. The evidence supports a useful asynchronous systems implementation, but not the manuscript’s claims of unchanged or comparable posterior quality, nor consistency of the algorithm actually run.

**Concrete Concerns**

- [MAJOR] §1 contribution 5 and §6.1, `sn-article.tex:57,316–327`: the repository directly falsifies “unchanged posterior quality throughout.” The paper reports \(0.07\)–\(0.08\) for every straggler-twin arm, but `experiments/scripts/make_twin_tables.py:91–94` selects only `final_quality_wasserstein`, the unweighted archive-to-truth diagnostic. In the same committed `tab_twin/twin_straggler_raw.csv`, the manuscript’s actual weighted estimator has median error \(0.0889\) for asynchronous versus \(1.1457\) for the coarse twin and \(1.8082\) for the fine twin at \(20\times\) slowdown. The honest analytic-posterior W1 is \(0.0087\) versus \(1.0665\) and \(1.7286\), respectively; the fine twin is already catastrophic at \(10\times\) (\(0.7372\) versus \(0.0113\)). `runtime_summary.py:248–349` explicitly identifies the weighted quantity as the reported posterior and the analytic-posterior distance as the honest metric. This is not a small discrepancy: the table reports the one metric that hides a failure present in two better metrics already stored beside it. Resolve by replacing every twin-quality claim with the weighted full-history and analytic-target results, explaining why the barrier schedule destabilizes the estimator, and rerunning under matched inference budgets. “Unchanged posterior quality” must be removed unless new experiments establish it.

- [MAJOR] §3.3 and §6.2, `sn-article.tex:121,419–420,504–521,588–595`: the paper labels an optimization diagnostic as posterior recovery. `experiments/async_abc/analysis/convergence.py:3–9,90–150` defines “Wasserstein” against the true parameter treated as a Dirac mass; in one dimension it is simply mean absolute deviation from the truth. This is not distance to the posterior and rewards an incorrectly collapsed distribution. For asynchronous runs, `convergence.py:412–525` applies it to the unweighted top-\(k\) archive; the synchronous arm uses its final generation (`:528–581`). The manuscript admits this at line 121, then calls the resulting comparison evidence that the “reported streaming posterior” is comparable at line 420 and that it “matches or exceeds” baseline posterior quality at line 595. The code itself acknowledges the defect at `runtime_summary.py:329–335`: the point-mass metric floors at posterior spread, whereas analytic-posterior W1 tends to zero. Resolve by evaluating each method’s actual reported weighted posterior against an analytic posterior or a high-accuracy reference distribution using W1/MMD/energy score, plus calibration or proper scoring rules. The present g-and-k, Lotka–Volterra, and Cellular Potts curves should be relabelled as top-\(k\) point-concentration diagnostics and cannot support posterior-quality claims.

- [MAJOR] Abstract, §1 contribution 2, §3.1, and conclusion, `sn-article.tex:41,54,91,119,595`: “stateless” and “a pure function of evaluated history” are false for the algorithm that generates the samples. The live propagator keeps `_snapshots` and `_calls_since_snapshot` (`../propulate/propulate/propagators/abcpmc.py:696–704`), uses the snapshots in the online importance denominator (`:1137–1162`), stores the resulting weight, and feeds those weights back into future proposal mixtures (`:856–907`). It also retains `_calls_since_bisect` and `_held_eps`, which determine when the tolerance is updated (`:1625–1629,1695–1715`). A restart clears or recreates these quantities and therefore changes subsequent weights, bandwidths, and proposals. Even the supposedly history-reconstructed tolerance preserves a cache-only minimum across migration, while the code admits that a restart can loosen it (`:279–304`). Calling this “performance state” does not make it statistically irrelevant. Resolve by either persisting/reconstructing all proposal-affecting state and demonstrating continuation equivalence under restart, or restricting the claim to “the post-hoc estimator is reconstructible from the retained history.” The algorithm itself is stateful.

- [MAJOR] Theorems 1–2 and Appendix A, `sn-article.tex:188–218,235–240,630–639,771–787`: the theorem does not establish consistency of the asynchronous estimator used in the experiments. The consistency conclusion is an \(r\)-tilted target under the assumed uniform fidelity condition; \(r=1\) is not proved, the positive limiting bandwidth is not established, and the asynchronous filtration is assumed. The implementation consumes an arrival-ordered log (`propulate_abc.py:727–749`), whereas the proofs use proposal order; the appendix explicitly states that transfer between the two is unproved (`sn-article.tex:637`). More seriously, if completion time depends on the simulated trajectory or discrepancy—as is plausible for Cellular Potts—deadline/arrival selection can change \(p(\rho\mid\theta)\), not merely the marginal draw density of \(\theta\). A theta-only ratio \(r(\theta)\) cannot absorb that distortion, despite §4.3 placing “asynchrony” wholly inside \(r\). The synthetic coupling experiment adds a post-evaluation delay determined by \(\theta\), so it does not test this failure mode. I found no clear algebraic error in the fixed-\(m\) proof conditional on its assumptions; the fatal issue is applicability. Resolve by retaining proposal IDs, proposal-time order, emitted proposal densities, simulator completion variables, and all launched jobs; then either prove a theorem for the joint \((\theta,\rho,T)\) selection law or use count-limited/drained experiments for which the conditional simulator law remains valid. Until then, “the estimator it reports is consistent” at `sn-article.tex:595` is not justified.

- [MAJOR] §6.1 and §6.3, `sn-article.tex:318–324,535–544`: the barrier twin does not isolate “nothing but” waiting in the sense claimed. `abcpmc_barrier.py:27–31` deliberately leaves information unbatched, while `:119–133` inserts a barrier before calls to the history-adaptive propagator. The barrier changes arrival histories and therefore realized proposals, even with the same proposal rule and random seeds; “identical proposals from identical seeds” at line 539 is unsupported. More importantly, `make_twin_tables.py:15–20` states that the arms are “never at a matched budget.” In the Cellular Potts data, asynchronous runs last about 1800 s and complete 16,797–129,572 evaluations, whereas twin runs last roughly 379–601 s and complete 2,016–15,360. Parameter-dependent simulator cost and adaptive proposals make rates from such different phases non-exchangeable. Every Cellular Potts `final_quality_wasserstein` entry is also missing, so there is no committed quality control for that twin. Resolve with a common fixed evaluation count for both arms, or record/replay an identical proposal stream with and without synchronization. Report all attempted jobs, including the five hangs, as time-to-result or failure outcomes.

- [MAJOR] §6.2, `sn-article.tex:420–430`: the higher-dimensional calibration narrative turns reporting support into a post-hoc tuning parameter without defining an estimator-selection rule. The text observes undercoverage for top-\(k\), overcoverage for full history, then selects \(M\approx400\)–\(800\) because it crosses nominal coverage. This establishes that coverage can be tuned after seeing SBC, not that a principled streaming posterior is calibrated. The claim that the effect is “not of the budget” rests on further uncommitted runs. Resolve by specifying \(M\), \(k\), and \(S\) before evaluation—preferably via an independent training/calibration split—and reporting held-out SBC across dimensions and models. “Conservative” is not a substitute for calibrated, especially when the reported support is an arbitrary knob.

- [MAJOR] Data availability, `sn-article.tex:613–620`: the current repository does not contain the raw records needed to reproduce the paper. I found 23 aggregate CSVs under `experiments/data/paper_figures`, but no per-particle result files, run databases, per-rank timing logs, or resolved job metadata. The twin-vendoring script itself reads hard-coded private scratch paths at `make_twin_tables.py:33–38`. Consequently, failed-run selection, deadline trimming, posterior reconstruction, uncertainty estimates, and most claimed diagnostics cannot be independently checked. “Will be deposited upon publication” is inadequate for a paper whose central defense depends on detailed post-hoc reweighting and scheduling semantics. Resolve by releasing immutable raw outputs and a one-command regeneration pipeline before review, not after acceptance.

- [MODERATE] §2.4, `sn-article.tex:85–86`: the positioning omits directly relevant parallel ABC work and overstates the synchronization gap. Schälte et al., [“A wall-time minimizing parallelization strategy for approximate Bayesian computation”](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0294015), explicitly use look-ahead execution to start next-generation ABC simulations before the current generation completes, discuss preliminary versus final proposals and reweighting, and analyze runtime-dependent sampling effects. That does not necessarily eliminate the narrower novelty of updating after every arrival, but it defeats the presentation that ABC frameworks simply retain a draining population barrier. Resolve by discussing this method technically, narrowing the novelty claim, and comparing against its look-ahead strategy or explaining why that comparison is impossible.

- [MODERATE] §6.1 runtime-coupling study, `sn-article.tex:382`: the claimed test of the theorem’s reported estimator actually reweights only a final archive. `benchmark_reports.py:103–113,129–165` calls `final_state_results`, whose asynchronous path hard-filters to `loss < epsilon_final` and then truncates to the archive (`final_state.py:89–113`), despite the smooth-kernel full-history estimator assigning nonzero mass to all evaluated records. This study therefore cannot show that the reported full-history estimator is robust to runtime coupling. Resolve by applying `posterior_weight` over every completed record, comparing censored and fully drained joint distributions, and including a runtime model dependent on simulated outcomes rather than only a deterministic delay after evaluation.

- [MODERATE] Analysis pipeline, `reporters.py:3818–3877`: the history-thinning implementation contradicts its own documentation. It promises that “posterior/quality use the full history,” but then overwrites `records` with the subsample at line 3876 before calling Gaussian posterior summaries, audits, corner plots, and quality routines. Histories over 200,000 records—precisely the fast Gaussian and straggler regimes—can therefore silently produce a “full-history” posterior from thinned data. Resolve by keeping separate `full_records` and `plot_records`, running every posterior/statistical summary on the former, and adding a regression test asserting equality with thinning enabled.

- [MODERATE] Appendix B, `sn-article.tex:806`: the reported speedups exclude the cost of producing the posterior. The manuscript itself says the retrospective pass takes about 20 minutes on the \(3\times10^7\)-particle Gaussian history, comparable to the timed simulation budget. That is not negligible time-to-inference, and no corresponding benchmark artifact is committed. Resolve by reporting simulation, online coordination, retrospective weighting, and total time-to-posterior separately for every benchmark. Throughput may remain a valid systems metric, but it must not be presented as end-to-end inference speed.

- [MODERATE] Figures 5 and Table 4, `sn-article.tex:410,527,543–557`: the synchronous scaling configuration is described incorrectly and the uncertainty claim is weak. The paper says population size equals worker count, but `make_scaling_combined_fig.py:18–23,47–71` uses \(k=100\) at 48 and 96 workers and only switches to \(k=W\) above that threshold. In addition, the synchronous 192/384-worker points have only three replicates, while “throughput unpredictability” is inferred from five twin replicates after five hanging jobs were retried. Resolve the caption/configuration mismatch, report effective simulator workers—the pyABC root coordinates rather than simulates (`pyabc_sampler.py:151–196`)—and include hangs and restart time in the reliability distribution with more replicates.

- [MINOR] Gaussian reference implementation, `gaussian_mean.py:70–81`: the analytic posterior mean under a bounded uniform prior is not generally the observed mean clipped to the prior bounds; it is the mean of a truncated normal. The sample generator at `:83–101` uses the correct truncated-normal law, so the two “analytic” references disagree near a boundary. The current truth appears far enough from the bounds that the numerical effect is probably negligible, but the implementation is mathematically wrong. Resolve by using `scipy.stats.truncnorm.mean` with the same parameters as the analytic sampler.

**Recommendation**

Reject. The work contains a credible and potentially publishable systems result—barrier-free execution materially improves utilization and throughput for expensive heterogeneous simulators—but the current manuscript turns that result into a statistical-method claim using metrics and controls that do not evaluate the estimator it defines. Most seriously, the committed twin data contradict a central abstract/introductory claim, so this cannot be repaired through wording alone. The single highest-leverage change is to rerun and rewrite the entire inference evaluation around the actual full-history weighted posterior, using genuine posterior references or proper scores and matched-budget barrier controls; only after that result is known should the theory and systems claims be rescaled to what the implementation demonstrably supports.
