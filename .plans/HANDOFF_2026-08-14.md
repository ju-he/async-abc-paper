## What the theory now says

Theorems are stated for the estimator the experiments report, not an idealized
variant. The algorithm's departures from the ideal are carried in one quantity:
`r`, the limiting ratio between the density the sample was drawn from and the
function the estimator divides by (Assumption 4(i), "fidelity").

- **Theorem 1 (consistency):** `pihat_n(f) -> pi^r(f)`, `pi^r ∝ pi L r`.
  `r == 1` is a corollary giving the smooth-ABC posterior.
- **Theorem 2 (CLT):** centred at `pi^r`, stable convergence, explicit variance
  `Z_r^-2 int pi^2 (f - pi^r f)^2 L^(2) tilde q_inf / qbar_inf^2`.
  **Scoped to damped/frozen-adaptation variants when the target is weakly
  identified** — see the stabilization finding below.
- **Corollary 3:** consistent variance estimator, per-run confidence intervals
  from stored weights. Not yet reported anywhere in the results — open.
- The prior floor in the *denominator* bounds every weight by `1/delta`
  **whatever the sampler does**; that is what removes the density-ratio
  condition a top-k proposal cannot satisfy.

## The stabilization finding (the substantive result of this session)

Assumption 4(ii) needs `||q_tau - q_inf|| ~ tau^-b` with `b > 1/2`. Measured by
rebuilding the proposal along stored histories:

| setting | b | verdict |
|---|---|---|
| serial 2-D Gaussian, identified target | 0.61–0.76 | passes with room |
| serial 2-D Gaussian, **confounded** (ridge) | 0.00–0.29 | fails |
| Cellular Potts production run (48 workers) | 0.14–0.33 | fails |

A confounded target reproduces the CPM failure **in a serial run**. So the
condition is one on the *inference problem*, not on the sampler or on
asynchrony. On a ridge the top-k archive slides along the flat direction
indefinitely and never settles.

Also measured and confirmed: the per-step drift decays as `tau^-0.92` and the
archive changes membership only 832 times in 33,748 draws, tracking `k log n`
(the record-statistics rate), so the snapshot clause is comfortably met.

**Two conjectures were written into the paper and then refuted by measurement:**
that the unpinned bandwidth caused the CPM failure (tested — pinning `min_tol`
moves `b` by <0.07, in both directions), and that asynchronous replay caused it
(superseded — a serial confounded run reproduces it). Do not re-propose either.

## CPM quality claims are scoped, at the author's direction

The CPM benchmark exists to exercise heterogeneous runtimes for the scaling
measurements. Its summaries, discrepancy metric and parameter ranges were never
selected for identifiability, and its two parameters confound. Quality figures
on it therefore bound no method's attainable accuracy. The abstract, results and
conclusion now say so. **Do not strengthen any CPM quality claim.**

## What was checked and rejected

- **Re-reporting posteriors at larger `m`** (would remove two of the four
  contributions to `r`): rejected on evidence, not cost. SBC writes only
  `sbc_trials.jsonl` (samples + weights), never ParticleRecords, so calibration
  cannot be re-derived. `propulate_abc.py:813-822` stamps a *running-minimum*
  tolerance on every record, destroying the bootstrap/archive split. And
  `raw_results.csv` is worker-tagged, not arrival-ordered. Stored CSVs cannot
  reproduce a run's own reported posterior even at unchanged `m`.
- Re-running SBC would cost **~0.55 node-hours** (measured from
  `sbc1000_20260729` timings) and needs only the existing
  `posterior_n_proposals` config field — no code change, sampler bit-identical.
  Not done: arrival-order nondeterminism means every SBC number would move.
